San Antonio federal judge again rules marketing texts count as "calls" (Waterman v. The General Automobile Insurance Services)

On November 16, 2026, a federal judge in San Antonio refused to dismiss a proposed class action over marketing texts from an auto insurer, ruling that texts count as "telephone calls" under the part of the federal telemarketing law that lets consumers bring their own Do Not Call lawsuits. The judge followed earlier Texas and Fifth Circuit-area rulings.

CaseWaterman v. The General Automobile Insurance Services, Inc.
CourtWestern District of Texas (W.D. Tex., 5th Circuit)
Docket / citationNo. 5:26-cv-03721-JKP, ECF No. 12
JudgeJason K. Pulliam, U.S. District Judge
DecidedNovember 16, 2026
Type of rulingRuling on a contested motion to dismiss (denied)
HoldingThe term "telephone call" in 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5) includes text messages, so telemarketing texts to a cell phone on the Do Not Call list can support a lawsuit. The court also rejected the insurer's other arguments for dismissal.

What happened in this case

Lauren Waterman sued The General Automobile Insurance Services, Inc. ("The General") as a proposed class action, meaning she asked to represent other people in a similar situation. She says The General sent multiple telemarketing text messages to her cell phone in June 2025 while her number was on the National Do Not Call Registry, and without her consent. According to the court, the texts introduced the sender as "The General Insurance," quoted her a price for insurance, gave her a "quote ID" and included a link. These are allegations; the court did not decide whether they are true.

The General asked the court to dismiss the case on several grounds, including that a text message is not a "telephone call" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), a 1991 federal law about telemarketing.

Key terms

  • Do Not Call list (National Do Not Call Registry): a free federal list where people can register their phone number to opt out of most telemarketing.
  • 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5): the part of the TCPA that lets a person who received "more than one telephone call within any 12-month period" from the same company, in violation of the telemarketing rules, bring their own lawsuit.
  • Motion to dismiss: a request asking the judge to end a case early because, even if everything the plaintiff says is true, the law does not give her a claim.
  • Established business relationship: under the Do Not Call rules, a company may contact someone it has a recent business relationship with, such as a recent purchase or inquiry.

What the court decided

Judge Jason K. Pulliam denied the motion on every ground.

  • Texts are calls. The judge noted "a growing split among district and circuit courts" but said he would not repeat that discussion because it is covered in an earlier Western District of Texas decision, Duron v. Kings Capital Holding. Pointing to rulings from Texas and elsewhere in the Fifth Circuit, including his own October 2026 ruling in Feliciano v. Red McCombs Hyundai Northwest, he concluded that "the term 'telephone call' incorporates text messages under the TCPA." He wrote that receiving telemarketing texts on a cell phone registered on the Do Not Call list is therefore actionable under § 227(c)(5).
  • The Do Not Call rules can be enforced in private lawsuits. The General argued, relying on a Middle District of Florida decision, Internicola v. MortgagePros, that § 227(c)(5) does not allow private lawsuits over the national Do Not Call rules because those rules were adopted in 2003. The judge called Internicola a "nonbinding, out-of-circuit district court decision" and declined to follow it.
  • Who sent the texts. The judge found the complaint plausibly alleged that The General itself sent the texts, based on their content.
  • Consent and business relationship. The General said the complaint did not properly allege that Waterman had no established business relationship with it and had not given permission. The judge said these are exemptions or defenses that the company must raise and prove, and that a plaintiff does not have to plead around them.
  • The judge also allowed Waterman's request for an injunction to go forward and said it was too early to strike the class allegations.

What it could mean in the Western District of Texas

The map on tcpamap.com shows the Western District of Texas in green (text treated as a call). This ruling is consistent with that status and with Judge Pulliam's earlier ruling in Feliciano, so it does not change the map.

This is not a finding that The General did anything wrong; the case now continues, and the company can still contest the facts and raise defenses. One trial judge's ruling does not bind other judges in the district. The Fifth Circuit, which covers Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi, has not decided this question, and the ruling could be reviewed on appeal.

The bigger picture

On July 14, 2026, the Seventh Circuit decided Steidinger v. Blackstone Medical Services, holding that § 227(c)(5) does not let people sue over texts. It is the only federal appeals court decision on this exact question so far, and it binds the federal courts in Illinois, Indiana and Wisconsin. Courts in the Ninth Circuit have declined to follow it, and other district courts have gone both ways.

Texas federal courts are now divided across districts. The Western District of Texas has continued to treat texts as calls. But on November 6, 2026, a Southern District of Texas judge held in Siringi v. Crust Pizza Co. that texts are not calls, leaving that district split, and on the same day as this order a Northern District of Texas judge reached the same result as Siringi in Starling v. What If Holdings.

The question opened up partly because of the Supreme Court's 2025 decision in McLaughlin Chiropractic Associates v. McKesson Corp., which said courts in private lawsuits do not have to follow the Federal Communications Commission's reading of the TCPA, which had long treated texts as calls. Courts now decide the meaning themselves.

As of November 18, 2026, the map on tcpamap.com shows 19 districts where a text was not treated as a call (red), 16 where it was (green), 2 where judges disagree (yellow) and 57 with no verified decision identified (grey), out of 94, counting the Northern District of Texas ruling issued the same day.
