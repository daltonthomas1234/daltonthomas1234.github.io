Marketing texts and the federal Do Not Call law

A federal law, the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, lets a person sue after more than one unwanted marketing contact in a year. The law calls that contact a “telephone call.” The rule is 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5).

Most of that marketing now arrives as a text. Courts do not all agree on whether a text counts. This page shows the written orders this review found and read. It is not a list of every case.

How a district gets a color

A district is colored only when this review retrieved the opinion or order and the document decides whether a text is a telephone call for the private claim in § 227(c)(5). The link under the district opens that document. Counts are the number of districts in each group. They are not a count of lawsuits.

A red or green district-court color means the orders point the same way. That order decides the case in front of that judge. It does not bind the other judges in the district. Where judges in one district have gone opposite ways, the map is yellow. The Southern District of Florida is in that group.

The Seventh Circuit color is different. Steidinger v. Blackstone Medical Services, No. 25-2398 (7th Cir. July 14, 2026), is a published appellate opinion. It applies in the district courts of Illinois, Indiana, and Wisconsin. Those districts are colored from that opinion, not from a local order. The district-court opinion in the appealed case was not separately downloaded.

Grey means no verified decision was identified in this review. It does not mean a court has held that no decision exists. An order that only assumes the answer, or that decides a different subsection, is not used for color.

Section 227(b), which covers certain automatic dialing and prerecorded calls, is a different question and is not mapped. An order is used whether or not the other side appeared, if the order decides whether a text is a telephone call under § 227(c)(5). If a later appellate opinion vacated or superseded a district order used here, the color would follow the later opinion. This review did not find that history for the orders that are colored.
